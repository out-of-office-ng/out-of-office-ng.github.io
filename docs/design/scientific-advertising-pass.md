# Scientific advertising pass · OOO 0x04

## Decision

Use specific, verifiable information in the invitation: what Out of Office is, the three past editions, what is currently known about 0x04, and what a visitor can do now. Keep the water and printed-paper voice. Sales stay closed.

This applies the useful parts of Claude C. Hopkins's *Scientific Advertising* (1923): a clear offer, concrete claims, and testing the response. The book is a historical source, not evidence that this particular page converts better. Source: [Library of Congress edition](https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/).

## What changed

- The hero now says what a visitor gets and sends them to real past editions. The removed destination displayed venue reference photos, not event coverage.
- The introduction and edition archive give the reason to believe: three named gatherings with recorded dates and locations. The venue-reference images are labelled as references, not event coverage.
- The 0x04 status and pass preview state the limits up front: November is planned; the exact date, venue, programme and pass details are pending; no passes are for sale.
- The owner chose to keep both the floating venue-photo field and photo showcase for their transitions. Both appear on the homepage; their current images remain labelled as references until replacement photos are approved.
- The mixtape now follows the essential event information as an optional part of the invitation.

## Questions to test with visitors

This change has no measured lift yet. Before claiming effectiveness, ask at least five people unfamiliar with the event to view the phone page and answer, without prompting:

1. What is Out of Office?
2. What do you know for certain about 0x04?
3. Can you buy a pass now?
4. Where would you go to see evidence of previous events?

Record answers and the width used. Watch whether visitors reach the archive and status without help. When a real waitlist or sale exists, compare one CTA wording change at a time using unique visits and completed signups or purchases; decide the success measure before a test starts. Do not present CTA clicks alone as sales.
